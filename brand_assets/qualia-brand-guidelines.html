Brand Guidelines

01 — Essence

What Qualia is, in one breath

A marketplace for personalized, one-on-one experiences, learning, and human connection.

People aren't paying for information — they're paying for a unique experience, and the chance to step temporarily into someone else's world. Every experience on Qualia is personalized and exclusive to the people who book it: not a class, not a tour group, not a video. One person, sharing something only they can teach, with another person, in a way that can't be replicated. Every part of the brand — the writing, the color, the mark — should feel like that: warm, specific, a little imperfect, and unmistakably human.

Human connection Authenticity Exclusivity Quality over quantity Safety Learning through real people

Paper carries the majority of every layout — Qualia should feel like warm paper, not a screen. Moss is the primary brand color: the logo, primary buttons, links, verified badges. Gold is a spark, not a base — one accent per view, used for ratings, highlights, and the aperture's warm inner glow. Rose is reserved strictly for safety and risk signals (waivers, higher-risk activity notices) — never used decoratively, so it keeps its meaning.

04 — Typography

Three typefaces, three jobs

FrauncesDisplay — headlines & pull quotes
Step into someone else's world.

Used big, and sparingly: page headlines, section titles, prices in hero contexts, and italic pull-quotes pulled from a host's own words. Never set as body text or in long paragraphs — Fraunces is a voice, not a paragraph face.

Light 300Rare — large hero moments only
Medium 500Default for headlines
SemiBold 600Emphasis, prices
ItalicPull quotes, taglines
InterBody — everything you read

Inter carries every paragraph, every button label, every form field, every line of navigation. It should feel invisible — clear, unfussy, and out of the way of the person's own words. Set body copy at 15–17px with generous line height (1.5–1.6) so it reads like something written by a person, not printed by a platform.

Reserved for category tags, eyebrows, prices in list contexts, timestamps, and metadata — anything that should read as data rather than a sentence. Set in uppercase with wide letter-spacing (about 0.05–0.09em) at small sizes (10–12px).

05 — The Aperture

One motif, used everywhere

The mark is a camera aperture — a ring that opens to reveal what's on the other side. That's the whole product: for one afternoon, you get access to a view you didn't have before. The motif recurs everywhere, always as a variation on the same two circles and eight blades, never redrawn from scratch.

List markers

A single thin ring replaces the bullet point in front of experience highlights and feature lists.

Thumbnail rings

Every host photo and experience thumbnail sits inside a thin aperture ring that tightens on hover.

The opening transition

When someone opens an experience, the aperture briefly expands full-screen — the literal moment of "stepping in."

06 — Voice & Tone

Write like a person, not a platform

WarmSpecificFirst-personUnpolished on purposeNever salesy
Do

"You're not learning a recipe. You're getting the one thing that's never made it into a cookbook."

Don't

"Learn authentic Italian cooking techniques from a professional chef in a fun, hands-on environment!"

Do

"I'm not running you through a class. I'm coaching you the way I was coached — one person, watched closely."

Don't

"Get fit fast with personalized training sessions tailored to your goals!"

Concretely: write in the host's own first-person voice wherever possible. Prefer one true sensory detail over three superlatives. "Amazing," "best-in-class," and exclamation points used to manufacture excitement are not part of the Qualia voice.
